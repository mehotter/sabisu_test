What the name means

サービス

sa · bi · su

Sabisu is how the word “service” is written and spoken in Japanese. We borrowed it because Japanese hospitality carries an idea we admire: notice what a guest will need, and attend to it before they have to ask.

That is the standard we train for. A good visit is one where you never have to point anything out.

Three things we believe

A short list is a promise

Every service we add divides our attention. Six is the number we can train for, equip for and inspect properly, so six is where we stop.

The person matters more than the product

Machines and solutions are easy to buy. Care is not. We hire slowly, pay fairly and keep our teams together, because the same hands do better work each time they return.

Leave nothing to be asked for

Cushions back in place. Switches wiped. The tap left dry. Small things nobody requests are how you can tell someone was paying attention.

Two hands, one circle

Our mark is two open hands. One offers and one receives, and together they close a circle around the name. It is a reminder of what a service really is: something passed carefully from one person to another.

The red seal beside it reads “sābisu” in Japanese script. We use the same seal to mark our guarantee.

Who comes to your home

You are opening your door to someone. You should know exactly who they are and how they got there.

Verified
Identity, address and police verification are completed for every professional before training begins.
Trained
Classroom training for their one service, followed by supervised visits alongside a lead. Nobody works alone until the lead signs them off.
Employed
Our professionals are on our payroll with steady hours and paid leave. They are not sent to you by an algorithm.
Identified
Before each visit you receive the names and photographs of the people coming. They arrive in uniform and carry a Sabisu identity card.
Insured
Accidental damage during a visit is covered. Tell the lead or call us, and we handle the claim.
